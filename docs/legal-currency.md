# Legal currency ledger

The gold sets are human legal judgement, and their rationales cite provisions. This
page records, for every legal proposition the harness itself states, the provision,
where it is used, what the repository says, the position in force on the date it was
checked, the official source that was read, and a status. It does not change any gold
answer: a row that needs a decision says so, and the decision stays with the owner.

`tests/test_legal_currency.py` keeps the table complete. It extracts every statutory
citation from the gold sets, the candidate drafts, the annotation pack, the stub
fixtures, the robustness campaigns, the contracts, the guides and the changelog, and
fails when one has no row here. Model output under `examples/` is not a repository
claim and is not scanned.

How to read a row:

- **Used in** lists `file:line` references as of the commit that last edited this page.
- **Verified on** is the date the official text was read. It is not a statement that
  nothing has changed since; the watch list below names what is pending.
- **Status** is `OK` (the statement matches the text in force), `WATCH` (correct today,
  with a pending change or case named in the watch list) or `NEEDS OWNER REVIEW` (new
  law, an omission or a framing question that only the owner can decide).

Source note for 2026-10-31: every EUR-Lex form of the GDPR, the Data Act, the DPF
decision and the Omnibus proposal (ELI, CELEX HTML and PDF) returned an empty HTTP 202
anti-bot challenge from the checking environment. The Official Journal texts were
therefore read from the Publications Office cellar manifestations that EUR-Lex itself
links to, which are the same OJ documents; the cellar URL is given beside the ELI.
Council documents were read from the Council public register, EDPB documents from
edpb.europa.eu, court records from curia.europa.eu and the BGB from
gesetze-im-internet.de.

## Watch list

Each item names what is pending, the rows it touches, and what should trigger a re-check.

1. **Digital Omnibus and GDPR Art. 33.** The Commission proposal COM(2025) 837 of
   19 November 2025 (procedure 2025/0360(COD), "Simplification of the digital
   legislative framework, Digital Omnibus") would replace Art. 33(1) GDPR: notification
   to the supervisory authority only for a breach likely to result in a high risk, "where
   feasible, not later than 96 hours". On 2026-10-31 the Legislative Observatory showed
   the file at "Awaiting committee decision" (ITRE and LIBE jointly responsible, draft
   report 22 June 2026, amendments 27 July 2026, no committee or plenary vote). The
   Council's mandate for negotiations, agreed by the Permanent Representatives Committee
   on 21 October 2026 and issued as document ST 14756/26 of 22 October 2026, keeps the
   96-hour deadline and the high-risk threshold. The Council press release of 21 October
   2026 could not be opened (HTTP 403), so its wording is unverified; the mandate text
   was read from the Council register. The "72 hour" statements stay correct until the
   amending regulation is published in the Official Journal. Re-check trigger: a plenary
   first-reading position, a trilogue agreement, and finally OJ publication; at that point
   re-read `expected/dpa.json:33`, the DPA draft, `fixtures/dpa_stub.json:19`,
   `robustness/campaign.v1.json:389` and the artefacts derived from them. Sources:
   https://oeil.europarl.europa.eu/oeil/en/procedure-file?reference=2025/0360(COD);
   https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-14756-2026-INIT/en/pdf;
   https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-15698-2025-INIT/en/pdf (the
   proposal as transmitted to the Council; EUR-Lex did not render).
2. **EU-US Data Privacy Framework, appeal C-703/25 P.** Pending before the Court of
   Justice against the General Court's judgment of 3 September 2025 in T-553/23; no
   judgment, opinion or hearing date on 2026-10-31. The transfers rationale does not
   rely on the framework, so a judgment either way leaves it correct; an annulment would
   still be worth a note in the row. This item was checked by one reader on
   curia.europa.eu; the second, independent re-check planned for it did not run.
   Re-check trigger: an Advocate General opinion or the judgment.
3. **EDPB Opinion 22/2024.** Adopted 7 October 2024; not cited beside
   `expected/dpa.json:29` and `expected/saas.json:37`, whose statements it supports.
   Whether to cite it is the owner's decision; the text does not change either rationale.
4. **Data Act Chapter VI and the SaaS export clause.** In force since 12 September
   2025 and not mentioned in the repository. The `data_export_on_termination` rationale
   calls thirty days with a standing export right the ordinary position; for an in-scope
   data processing service it is a statutory minimum (Art. 25(2)(g)). The ninety-day
   notice in the `auto_renewal` clause may also interact with the two-month cap on the
   switching notice period (Art. 25(2)(d)); that interaction is contested and is not
   concluded here. Whether, and how, to change the SaaS gold set is the owner's decision.
   The SaaS and DPA candidate drafts carry `legal_position_date` 2026-09-04, after the
   Data Act's application date, and do not address it; the NDA draft carries 2026-10-27.

## Maintenance

- Add a row before adding a citation. The test names the file and the citation it
  could not find; the first column of the new row must contain the citation as the test
  spells it (`Art. 28(3)(d)`, `Article 28` becomes `Art. 28`, `section 311 (1) ... BGB`
  becomes `§ 311(1) BGB`, `lit. g` in DPA material becomes `Art. 28(3)(g)`).
- Re-verify a row when its watch-list item fires, and update **Verified on** and the
  source whenever the official text is re-read, even if nothing changed.
- Keep legal decisions out of this page: it records what the law says and where the
  repository relies on it. Severities, flags and rationales change only in the gold
  sets, by the owner.
