# Contracting playbook for hosted services and data processing (SYNTHETIC)

Owner: Legal, Northwind Materials GmbH
Version: 3, in force since 1 July 2026

This playbook is fabricated for evaluation purposes. It is not a statement of
the law and not a recommendation for any real contract.

## How to read a rule

Each rule states a preferred position, a fallback and a limit.

- Accept: the term meets the preferred position.
- Negotiate: the term misses the preferred position, and a position at or better
  than the fallback is still open. Propose the preferred position.
- Reject: the term is beyond the limit. Northwind does not sign it as drafted.
- Escalate: the decision belongs to someone above the Head of Legal, or the
  information needed to apply the rule is missing.

A counterparty offer that meets the fallback but not the preferred position is
accepted, and the acceptance is recorded as a fallback approval by the Head of
Legal.

## PB-01 Contracting party and order of precedence

Preferred: one identified legal entity is the counterparty in every deal
document, and the Data Processing Agreement prevails over the main agreement on
data protection matters.
Fallback: affiliated entities may sign different documents if each is named, and
the main contracting party is liable for the affiliate's performance.
Limit: escalate while the identity of the entity that operates the service is
unknown.

## PB-02 Use of Northwind data by the vendor

Preferred: the vendor uses Northwind data only to provide the service.
Fallback: the vendor may use statistics that are irreversibly anonymised and
that cannot be used to create products identifying Northwind. There is no
fallback for personal data.
Limit: reject any licence or retention right that covers personal data.

## PB-03 Sub-processors

Preferred: 30 days' prior written notice of a new sub-processor, a right to
object, and a right to terminate without penalty if the objection is not
resolved.
Fallback: 14 days' prior written notice with a right to object.
Limit: reject appointment without prior notice and reject any waiver of the
right to object.
Reference: Art. 28(2) and 28(3)(d) GDPR.

## PB-04 Data location and transfers

Preferred: storage and access within the EEA only.
Fallback: remote access from a third country is permitted where the Data
Processing Agreement names the transfer mechanism and the vendor has supplied
its transfer impact assessment.
Limit: escalate any access to employee data from outside the EEA, because the
works council must be informed before signature.
Reference: Chapter V GDPR.

## PB-05 Notification of personal data breaches

Preferred: notification within 24 hours of the vendor becoming aware.
Fallback: notification within 48 hours.
Limit: escalate any period longer than 48 hours. Reject any period longer than
72 hours.
Reference: Art. 28(3)(f) and Art. 33 GDPR.

## PB-06 Audit

Preferred: an on-site audit once a year and an additional audit for cause.
Fallback: a third-party audit report every year, plus an on-site audit for cause
after a security incident or at the request of a supervisory authority.
Limit: reject a complete exclusion of on-site audits.
Reference: Art. 28(3)(h) GDPR.

## PB-07 Liability

Preferred: a general cap of 200% of the annual fees, with unlimited liability
for breaches of data protection, confidentiality and intellectual property
indemnities.
Fallback: a separate cap of 500% of the annual fees for breaches of data
protection and confidentiality.
Limit: escalate anything below the fallback. Where special categories of
personal data are processed, the fallback is not available and every liability
position is escalated.

## PB-08 Term and renewal

Preferred: a fixed term without automatic renewal.
Fallback: automatic renewal with a notice period of 30 days or less.
Limit: negotiate any longer notice period. An initial term of up to 24 months is
accepted.

## PB-09 Fees

Preferred: fees fixed for the initial term, later increases limited to 3% per
year, and a right to terminate on any increase.
Fallback: later increases limited to 5% per year.
Limit: reject an uncapped right to increase fees. Escalate a cap above 5%.

## PB-10 Exit and deletion

Preferred: export in a standard format, 90 days of access after termination, a
deletion certificate and no retention by the vendor.
Fallback: 30 days of access after termination.
Limit: reject retention of Northwind data for the vendor's own purposes.
Reference: Art. 28(3)(g) GDPR.

## PB-11 Service levels

Preferred: a binding uptime commitment of 99.5% or higher with service credits.
Fallback: a binding commitment of 99.5% without service credits.
Limit: negotiate any commitment that is only an efforts undertaking.

## PB-12 Security

Preferred: the vendor's security questionnaire answers are attached as a
schedule and are binding.
Fallback: a described set of technical and organisational measures is attached.
Limit: negotiate a clause that refers only to a general standard. Escalate while
the description of measures has not been received.
Reference: Art. 28(3)(c) and Art. 32 GDPR.

## PB-13 Indemnities

Preferred: the vendor indemnifies Northwind against third-party intellectual
property claims concerning the service.
Fallback: mutual indemnities, each party for its own materials.
Limit: reject any indemnity under which Northwind bears claims concerning the
vendor's own service.

## PB-14 Confidentiality agreements

Preferred: Northwind's own non-disclosure agreement.
Fallback: a vendor-paper agreement that is replaced by the confidentiality
clause of the main agreement at signature.
Limit: reject a residual knowledge clause that permits use for third parties,
and reject assignment to a competitor without consent. A vendor-paper agreement
that is already signed is escalated if it contains a rejected term.

## PB-15 Governing law and venue

Preferred: German law and the courts of Frankfurt am Main.
Fallback: the law of another EEA member state.
Limit: negotiate a document that is silent on governing law. Escalate any law
outside the EEA.
